Back to work Case study 01 · Agoda

One email system for 13+ markets, and an AI pipeline to ship it.

I led the design of Agoda's modular email design system and the production pipeline behind it, taking campaigns from Figma to HTML, through Litmus testing, to the ESP, with AI handling the repetitive steps.

01The challenge

Every campaign was rebuilt by hand, in every language.

Agoda sends lifecycle and campaign emails to travelers and hotel partners across Asia and beyond. Each product (hotels, flights, activities, cross-sell) had its own look, each market needed its own copy, and every email had to survive Outlook as well as modern clients.

Without a shared system, designers rebuilt layouts from scratch, developers re-coded the same blocks, and localization meant duplicating files one language at a time. Consistency suffered and the team couldn't scale personalization.

02The approach

Design the system, then automate the system.

  1. Break emails into a fixed anatomyEvery email became header, banner, body and footer. Each zone got its own component variants, so a new campaign starts by duplicating one template rather than a blank file.
  2. Build tokens for how email actually rendersEmail clients don't load custom fonts, so the system uses Nunito from Google Fonts as the closest open match to Agoda's typeface. Buttons, spacing and type sizes are fixed, and each product has its own colour theme.
  3. Give each audience its own libraryThe Customer Marketing library covers B2C (price alerts, trip savings, cross-sell). The Partner Communications library covers B2B, with 23 header variants and body layouts. The rule was simple: find the component and name it for the developer, or escalate to me and we add it to the library.
  4. Turn the hand-off into a pipelineI spearheaded the production flow below, with Make and Vertex AI automating the repetitive steps between design and send.
  5. Localize from one sourceFor campaign assets, copy lives in one Google Sheet with a tab per language. A Figma plugin syncs each language into its template set, so one master design produces every market's version.
Figma HTML Litmus ESP

03The solution

Two libraries, one pipeline, every market.

For B2C, banners, product cards and footers snap together into personalized emails: price drops, trip savings, "complete your trip" cross-sell. For B2B, the Partner Communications library gives the team a defined colour theme per product and a step-by-step guide, so non-designers can assemble on-brand emails and popups.

For campaigns, the Pulse template set takes one key visual and turns it into every format (email banner, app popup, homepage banner, LINE ads) for 15 market sets, including Arabic.

04The impact

Faster, more consistent, and ready for personalization.

  • 50% faster production once the Figma → HTML → Litmus → ESP pipeline was in place.
  • 200+ personalized campaigns a month, localized for 13+ languages and markets.
  • One source of truth for Customer Marketing and Partner Communications, so designers and developers worked from the same components and names.
  • A base for hyper-personalization: the same system powered retargeting emails with review summaries and price advisors, which contributed to a $30K+ daily long-term contribution gain.

The goal was never just prettier emails. It was making the right email for the right traveler cheap enough to produce that personalization could scale.

05What I learned

Systems only scale when the process does.

The components mattered, but adoption came from the guides, the naming rules and the escalation path. Writing the "how" as clearly as the "what" let teams outside design ship on-brand work without waiting for me.
